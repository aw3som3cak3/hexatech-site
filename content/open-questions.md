18. ~~**Frame material selection**~~ — resolved by [ADR-0041](adr/0041-frame-material-titanium-gr5-and-anodize-finish.md): Ti Gr5 for field grade (bead-blast ring, anodize-blue core); 316L stainless acceptable for PoC and training variants.
19. ~~**Chip A standalone data scope**~~ — resolved by [ADR-0038](adr/0038-chip-a-ring-only-profile-red-flag-and-locator.md): chip A holds a ring-only profile — red-flag line and tag locator only. No full Tier 1 duplicate.
20. ~~**NOK delegated write access**~~ — resolved by [ADR-0039](adr/0039-nok-delegated-write-deferred.md): not supported in Phase A; preferred long-term path is account-level delegation once the account/identity layer is specified.
21. ~~**Death verification relay mechanism**~~ — resolved for the card form factor by [ADR-0063](adr/0063-death-confirmation-card-form-factor.md): single JCOP applet holds both signatures; no relay required. Remains open for the ring form factor if that design is revisited.
22. **Offline certificate expiry without a trusted clock** (ADR-0004): the chip tracks the latest date seen in a terminal certificate (ratchet clock). An adversary who steals a medic's phone and credentials while they are valid, then keeps the phone offline, may be able to use those credentials indefinitely — the chip has never seen a later date, so the certificate has not expired from the chip's perspective. Read caps (ADR-0005) mitigate per-tag exposure but do not bound how many different tags a compromised reader can access offline. This gap needs an explicit threat model entry and a mitigation decision (e.g. short-lived terminal certs, online revocation check on first use after a gap, or explicit acceptance of the residual risk).
23. ~~**Short-ID as a permanent physical correlation vector**~~ — resolved by [ADR-0060](adr/0060-identifier-model-enrollment-id-pairwise-rp-id-session-token.md): Tag Locator is derived from Enrollment ID (not chip UUID); trade-off explicitly accepted — physical identification requires a stable visible ID; NFC-layer protections (ADR-0024/0036) operate in a separate threat model.
24. **Tamper evidence and state communication** *(ring form factor — ADR-0042, ADR-0052)*: the visual design signals how the tag separates (open front face, parting groove) but does not communicate whether it has already been separated, is safe to separate, or has been tampered with. A colour distinction between ring and core is not, by itself, a tamper-evidence mechanism. Should the design encode a visible separation indicator (e.g. a one-time element that cannot be restored after separation)? *Card form factor equivalent: does the grommet + tear-away design need to encode a visible separation state?*
25. **Three operational states not formally specified** *(ring form factor — ADR-0052)*: the tag has three distinct operational states — intact (full digital + physical ID available), separated (ring with casualty, core with reporting chain), and damaged/incomplete (electronic layer absent, physical engraving + UUID cylinder only). No ADR formally defines these states or specifies the design requirements that follow from each. *Card form factor equivalent: intact (tag + tear-away), separated (tag on chain / tear-away with reporting party), electronic-failure (tag destroyed, tear-away only).*

26. **Grommet post-fire validation — three distinct claims** (ADR-0056): the 316L grommet fire-survival argument conflates three separate properties that must be validated independently: (a) 316L stainless steel survives temperatures that destroy the plastic card — this follows from melting points; (b) the engraved UUID identifier remains *legible* after realistic fire exposure, including soot, oxidation, heat distortion and abrasion — this requires a physical test on a production grommet with an engraved UUID; (c) the grommet remains *attached to the chain* after the card burns away — the press-fit or crimped retention must survive the same conditions without the card body to hold it in place. Claims (b) and (c) are not established by material properties alone. Physical test protocol required before treating the grommet as an adequate fire-survival fallback. Resolve by ADR before ADR-0056 is accepted.

27. **First product scope — medical continuity vs. field authentication vs. combination** (strategy.md): the card design enables both emergency medical identification and COTS field authentication (ADR-0044). These are potentially separate products with different buyers, certification paths, and regulatory requirements. The combined offering may be harder to procure than either standalone. Resolve by defining the minimum viable product scope for the first deployment: (a) emergency medical ID only, (b) field authentication only, or (c) a deliberately scoped combination with explicit buyer identification. This determines the certification priority and the sales motion. Extends OQ-1.

28. **Two-buyer procurement path** (strategy.md): military medical services and military IT/security organisations may have separate budgets, qualification processes, and procurement timelines. The strategy assumes the medical ID gets Hexa procured while authentication keeps it funded, but this requires either a single buyer controlling both decisions or a coordinated procurement across two separate organisations. Validate whether the target nations (Försvarsmakten/UK/Germany) handle authentication tokens and medical ID through the same procurement authority, or separate ones. Resolve before the sales strategy is finalised.

29. **Antenna RF validation with steel grommet** (ADR-0056): the two-turn coil and ≥2 cm read distance are stated as design targets. The 316L grommet in the chain hole introduces a metal element in close proximity to the antenna. Antenna inductance, tuning and read range must be validated by RF testing on a physical prototype, not assumed from the coil geometry alone. The number of turns, coil dimensions and tuning capacitor values should be treated as outputs of the RF test, not inputs to it. Resolve before Gate 1 (ADR-0052).

30. **Consumer-mode attribution gap** (ADR-0061, Decision 1a): consumer Tier 1 reads are unattributed. The chip-signed response proves the data came from a known enrolled chip, but the reader remains anonymous; no mutual signature or log delivery is guaranteed. ADR-0061 takes an explicit P1 exception. Resolve by: (a) accepting the gap with deployment authority sign-off, (b) defining a best-effort attribution path (e.g., voluntary reader signature if Hexa app is installed), or (c) defining when consumer deployments require the Hexa app (which can submit signed receipts). Links to OQ-32.

31. **Consumer-mode casualty log append policy** (ADR-0062, casualty log domain): in consumer mode there is no terminal PKI. The casualty log domain specifies that append access in consumer mode is unresolved. Options: (a) require the Hexa reader app credential to append (limits genuine bystander scenario but prevents junk entries), (b) allow unsigned appends from any reader (risks log pollution), (c) disallow appends in consumer mode entirely (reduces utility). Resolve in the applet specification ADR.

32. **NFC interaction model: Hexa app required vs NDEF fallback** (ADR-0061, Decision 1b): iOS devices cannot read a custom ISO 7816 applet response without an installed app declaring the AID. The "bystander with any NFC phone" consumer scenario requires either (a) the Hexa app is installed (limits reach), or (b) an NDEF fallback record exposes Tier 1 data at the NDEF layer (accessible to any iOS device without an app, but with different security and format constraints). Decide before the applet specification ADR is written.

33. **Steel tear-away NFC field shielding — Gate 1 test required** (ADR-0056): The 316L stainless tear-away (54 mm across-corners, 2.5 mm thick) is a large solid conducting disc. When the tag and tear-away are worn stacked together on the neck chain, the steel disc attenuates the 13.56 MHz NFC field via eddy currents — a significantly larger effect than the 9 mm grommet (OQ-29). Reads may fail in the stacked configuration. This is a hardware blocker that must be characterised on a physical prototype before Gate 1. Options: (a) ferrite sheet between the elements, (b) an orientation doctrine requiring the reader to present to the card face with the tear-away oriented away, or (c) a guaranteed standoff (separate chain length keeps elements apart during reads). Note: "unclip tear-away first" is excluded as an option — the tear-away doctrine (ADR-0063 Decision 1b) limits separation to confirmed KIA; a wounded person cannot unclip their own tear-away. Resolve by physical testing at Gate 1 (ADR-0052); document the mitigation in an ADR amendment before ADR-0056 is accepted.
